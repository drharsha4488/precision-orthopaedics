# Rotator Cuff Repair Recovery Time

Recovering from a rotator cuff repair requires patience and strict adherence to a rehabilitation protocol. Because the tendon needs time to biologically heal to the bone, rushing the process can jeopardize the repair.

## Phase 1: Maximum Protection (Weeks 0-6)
Immediately following surgery, your arm will be immobilized in a specialized sling. The goal during this phase is to protect the repair while preventing the shoulder from becoming completely stiff.
*   **Activity:** You will perform gentle "pendulum" exercises as directed by Dr. Deepthi Nandan Reddy.
*   **Restrictions:** No active lifting, reaching, or driving.

## Phase 2: Active Assisted Motion (Weeks 6-12)
Once the initial biological healing has occurred, the sling is discontinued. 
*   **Activity:** You will begin moving the arm using your own muscle power, often assisted by pulleys or your other arm.
*   **Focus:** Restoring full range of motion without bearing weight.

## Phase 3: Strengthening (Months 3-5)
With motion restored, the focus shifts to rebuilding the weakened muscles.
*   **Activity:** Resistance bands and light weights.
*   **Milestones:** Most patients return to normal daily activities (like reaching overhead into cabinets) during this phase.

## Phase 4: Return to Sport/Heavy Activity (Months 6+)
For athletes or heavy laborers, this final phase involves sports-specific kinetic profiling (often using our VALD Performance lab) to ensure the shoulder can handle high-velocity stress.
