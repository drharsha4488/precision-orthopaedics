## 1. The basic problem

Traumatic anterior glenohumeral dislocation is the most common large-joint dislocation.
Recurrence after a first dislocation managed non-operatively is very common in young,
active patients (often reported as >50–90% in patients under 20–25). Recurrent
instability causes:

1. **Soft-tissue injury.** The anteroinferior labrum and IGHL are detached (**Bankart
   lesion**). Variants are ALPSA, Perthes, GLAD and HAGL.
2. **Glenoid bone loss (GBL).** Bony Bankart or attritional erosion produces the
   "inverted pear" glenoid.
3. **Humeral bone loss.** The **Hill-Sachs lesion (HSL)** is a posterosuperolateral
   impaction fracture of the humeral head.

**Bipolar bone loss** is glenoid and humeral loss together. It decides which operation
succeeds, and it is the central idea of this paper.

## 2. The three procedures

| | **Arthroscopic Bankart repair (ABR)** | **Bankart + Remplissage (ABR+R)** | **Latarjet (open / arthroscopic)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principle | Re-attach labrum/IGHL to the glenoid rim with suture anchors and restore capsular tension | ABR **plus** capsulotenodesis of the posterior capsule and infraspinatus into the HSL ("fills" the defect so it becomes extra-articular) | Transfer of the coracoid process with the conjoint tendon to the anteroinferior glenoid |
| Fixes | Soft tissue only | Soft tissue + humeral side (HSL engagement) | Glenoid bone loss (**bone block**), **sling effect** of the conjoint tendon (dynamic, especially in abduction–ER), and **capsular repair** to the CA-ligament stump: the *triple-blocking effect* (Patte) |
| Original description | Bankart 1923/1938 | Purchase, Wolf et al. 2008 (concept: Connolly 1972) | Latarjet 1954; popularised by Walch & Boileau |
| Typical indication | On-track HSL, GBL < ~13.5%, low ISIS | **Off-track** HSL with GBL below the critical threshold (< ~15–20%) | GBL ≥ ~20% (critical), off-track HSL with subcritical GBL, high ISIS, revision after failed Bankart, collision athletes |
| Main downsides | Higher recurrence in high-risk patients | Small loss of external rotation (usually a few degrees), posterior shoulder pain early, debate about function in overhead throwers | Non-anatomic, steeper learning curve, nerve injury (musculocutaneous/axillary), nonunion, graft osteolysis, hardware problems; overall complication rate ~15–30% in reviews; late arthropathy if graft is lateral/overhanging |

## 3. Key concepts and measurements the paper must use

### 3.1 Glenoid bone loss (GBL)
- **Best-fit circle method** on the en-face 3D-CT (or MRI) sagittal glenoid view:
  `GBL % = defect width (d) / diameter of best-fit circle (D) × 100`.
- **Critical GBL.** The historical threshold is ~20–25% (Itoi 2000; Burkhart & De Beer 2000
  "inverted pear"). Above it, isolated Bankart fails often.
- **Subcritical GBL.** Shaha et al. (AJSM 2015) showed that **> 13.5%** GBL gave clinically
  worse WOSI scores after Bankart repair, even without frank recurrence. The 13.5–20%
  zone is the current grey zone, and the STABLE and OASIS trials are testing it.

### 3.2 Hill-Sachs lesion and the glenoid track (Yamamoto 2007; Di Giacomo, Itoi & Burkhart 2014)
- **Glenoid track (GT) = 0.83 × D − d.** D is the intact glenoid diameter and d is the
  anterior glenoid defect width.
- **Hill-Sachs interval (HSI)** is the HSL width plus the bone bridge between the
  rotator-cuff footprint and the lateral edge of the HSL.
- **On-track:** HSI < GT. **Off-track:** HSI > GT. An off-track lesion is an engaging
  lesion that needs to be addressed (remplissage or Latarjet).
- **Distance-to-dislocation (DTD) = GT − HSI** (Li et al., ⚠️ verify). A small DTD
  (< ~8–10 mm) in an on-track shoulder ("near-track" / **peripheral-track**, Yamamoto)
  carries a higher Bankart failure risk. This gives a continuous variable rather than a
  binary one, and is a good secondary analysis.

### 3.3 Instability Severity Index Score (ISIS; Balg & Boileau, JBJS Br 2007): 10 points
| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Age at surgery < 20 years | 2 |
| Competitive sport (vs recreational/none) | 2 |
| Contact or forced-overhead sport | 1 |
| Shoulder hyperlaxity (ER at side > 85° or inferior hyperlaxity) | 1 |
| Hill-Sachs visible on AP radiograph in external rotation | 2 |
| Loss of the sclerotic inferior glenoid contour on AP radiograph | 2 |

In the original series, Bankart recurrence was 5% for ISIS ≤ 3 and 70% for ISIS > 6.
External validation has been inconsistent, so the paper should report ISIS but **not rely
on it alone**. Modified versions add CT-based bone loss (e.g. GIS / "ISIS-CT").

### 3.4 Outcome instruments
- **Recurrence:** dislocation, subluxation, positive apprehension, revision for
  instability. Report each one separately and also as a composite.
- **PROMs:** **WOSI** (instability-specific, 0–2100, lower = better; also reported as %),
  **Rowe score** (0–100), **ASES**, **SANE**, **VAS pain**.
- **ROM:** forward flexion, **ER at side (ER1)**, **ER in 90° abduction (ER2)**. ER2 is the
  key comparison for remplissage.
- **Return to sport:** yes/no, same level, time to return.
- **Arthropathy:** **Samilson–Prieto** grade on radiographs, preoperatively and at final
  follow-up.
- **Latarjet-specific:** graft union, osteolysis, and graft position on postop CT
  (flush / medial / lateral overhang; height relative to the glenoid equator).

### Take-home synthesis
1. **Isolated Bankart** works well for the "simple" shoulder (on-track, GBL < 13.5%, low
   ISIS). It fails in bipolar bone loss, young collision athletes and off-track lesions,
   and its failure rate rises with longer follow-up.
2. **Remplissage** turns many off-track shoulders into a Bankart success. Recurrence is
   comparable to Latarjet in pooled data, at the cost of a small ER deficit. Its role in
   **subcritical GBL (13.5–20%) with an off-track HSL** is the main unresolved question.
3. **Latarjet** gives the lowest recurrence, especially with critical GBL and in
   revision. It costs more complications and needs careful graft positioning to avoid
   arthropathy.
4. **Gap the paper can fill:** real-world comparative data that stratify all three
   procedures by **GBL %, glenoid-track status/DTD and ISIS**, with standard PROMs
   (WOSI/Rowe), ER2 and recurrence. Indian/South-Asian cohorts in particular are
   under-represented in this literature.
