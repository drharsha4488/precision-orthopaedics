# Bankart + remplissage vs Bankart alone vs Latarjet: evidence and benchmarks by bone loss

Literature search date: 2026-10-07. Source: PubMed (all PMIDs/DOIs below were retrieved from PubMed in this search). Numbers are taken from the abstracts; full texts were not read. Check them against the full text before you cite them in the manuscript.
Context: retrospective cohort, arthroscopic Bankart (BRUMA Mason-Allen technique) + Hill-Sachs remplissage, including subcritical glenoid bone loss (GBL).

---

## 1. Summary of the evidence

1. **Level-1 evidence that remplissage lowers recurrence.** There is one double-blind multicentre RCT (MacDonald et al., n=108, GBL <15%, engaging Hill-Sachs). It was published in **JSES 2021**, not JBJS. Two-year recurrent dislocation was 18% after Bankart alone vs 4% after Bankart + remplissage. Revisions were 6 vs 0. PROMs (WOSI, ASES) did not differ at 2 years. At a mean of about 4 years the gap persisted: redislocation 22% vs 8% and overall instability 30% vs 10% (Woodmass 2024). A secondary analysis found the benefit concentrated in Hill-Sachs lesions that extend inferiorly (lower-edge angle >90°: 36.7% vs 7.1%).
2. **Meta-analyses agree.** Pooled odds ratios for recurrence after Bankart alone vs Bankart + remplissage range from about **4 to 9**. Revision OR is about 3.4–7.7. Return to sport is equal or better with remplissage. External-rotation loss is about 1–6° and neither statistically nor clinically significant (Villarreal-Espinosa 2024; Ahmed 2024; Pawłuś 2024; Gonzalez-Morgado 2025).
3. **Benefit extends to on-track lesions.** In "near-track" lesions (distance-to-dislocation <8–10 mm) and in contact athletes, adding remplissage cut recurrence from 66.7% to 4.2% (Lin 2023). A Markov model found remplissage cost-effective for on-track lesions (Frederickson 2026).
4. **Bankart + remplissage vs Latarjet.** Pooled comparisons (6–12 cohort studies, about 800–1,200 patients) show **no significant difference in recurrence, revision, PROMs or ER**. Complications are significantly lower with remplissage: **1.6% vs 7.7%** (Deshpande 2025; Nong 2026). However, an AMSTAR-2 appraisal rated 10 of the 13 reviews "critically low" quality, and 12 of 13 abstracts contained spin (Olaonipekun 2026). Level-1 data do not exist yet. The STABLE RCT (NCT05705479; GBL 10–20%, n=114, primary outcome WOSI) is ongoing.
5. **Where remplissage appears to reach its limit.** This is the key risk for the hypothesis. In a large off-track cohort (Yang 2018, n=189), recurrence with GBL >15% was **28.6% after remplissage vs 6.1% after Latarjet**. Remplissage was also worse in contact/collision athletes and in revision surgery. Against that, a smaller series of 47 patients with mean GBL of about 25% had no redislocations in either arm at 2 years (Horinek 2022, ORTHOP J SPORTS MED).
6. **Isolated Bankart degrades within the subcritical range.** With isolated Bankart, both WOSI and recurrence worsen from about 13.5% GBL upward (Shaha 2015; Dickens 2017). The glenoid track (on/off) predicts failure better than GBL alone: off-track 75% failure vs on-track 8% (Shaha 2016). Contemporary pooled recurrence after isolated arthroscopic Bankart is about **12–13%**, higher in contact athletes (17%) and with ISIS ≥4 (26%).
7. **What this means for your study.** A BRUMA + remplissage cohort with recurrence of about 5–8% and WOSI change above the MCID in the 13.5–25% GBL band would be a new finding. The best existing comparator data for that band are Yang 2018 and Horinek 2022, and both use Latarjet as the comparator.

## 3. Thresholds and indications (current thinking)

- **Glenoid track (on/off).** The glenoid track concept comes from a cadaveric study (Yamamoto 2007). The medial margin of the track lies about 84% of glenoid width from the cuff footprint. Di Giacomo, Itoi and Burkhart (2014) turned this into a treatment paradigm: an off-track lesion engages and needs to be addressed. In the commonly cited algorithm, an on-track lesion with GBL <25% gets Bankart alone, an off-track lesion with GBL <25% gets Bankart + remplissage, and GBL ≥25% gets Latarjet. This wording is my paraphrase of the paradigm, which is described in PMID 24384275 and 26704802. Check the exact categories in the full text.
- **Critical vs subcritical GBL.** The historical critical threshold was 20–25% GBL. Shaha 2015 showed WOSI deteriorating to an unacceptable level above **13.5%**, even in shoulders that did not redislocate. Dickens 2017 showed all football players with GBL >13.5% failed after isolated Bankart. A review of risk factors found the most predictive GBL thresholds were **10–15%** on advanced imaging (Trasolini 2021). The working subcritical band is now roughly **13.5–20/25%**. STABLE uses 10–20%, and Hoyt/Dickens 2024 describe it as about 13.5–20%.
- **Peripheral-track and near-track lesions.** Yamamoto 2023 classes Hill-Sachs occupancy ≥75% of the glenoid track as "peripheral-track". Li 2021 defines "near-track" as distance-to-dislocation <8 mm, a cutoff that predicts failure after Bankart. The Pittsburgh group uses <10 mm (Lin 2023; Charles 2025, PIT score, where remplissage lowered the score by 8 points).
- **Bone loss that Bankart + remplissage has been shown to tolerate:**
  - RCT data (MacDonald, Woodmass) cover only GBL **<15%**.
  - In off-track cohorts, remplissage did about as well as Latarjet at GBL 10–15% (OR 1.28) but clearly worse at **>15%** (recurrence 28.6% vs 6.1%) (Yang 2018).
  - A small favourable series reached a mean GBL of about 25%, with n=22 and 2-year follow-up (Horinek 2022).
  - Deshpande 2025 concludes there is "likely a limit" of GBL for remplissage but does not quantify it.
- **Patient factors that favour bone block regardless of GBL:** contact/collision sport, revision surgery (Yang 2018), seizure disorder (recurrence 48% after soft-tissue repair vs 13% after bone block; Barry 2025, PMID 41737264), ISIS ≥4 (Rosenberg 2021), and age <20 (Sánchez Cruz 2026).
- **ISIS.** The original paper suggested Latarjet for a score >6 (Balg & Boileau 2007). Later systematic reviews found a cutoff of 2–4 discriminates better (Trasolini 2021; Rosenberg 2021).

- **Typical SD.** In Elamo 2022, WOSI (%) SD was 20.3 preoperatively and 15.5 at 12 months, and the change score SD was 24.5. For planning, assume SD ≈ 20% (about 420 raw points), with a sensitivity analysis at 25%.
- **Sample sizes** (two-sided α 0.05, power 80%, independent t-test on WOSI %):
  - Δ = 12.8, SD 20: **39 per group**
  - Δ = 12.8, SD 25: **60 per group**
  - Δ = 10, SD 20: **63 per group**
  - Δ = 15, SD 20: **28 per group**
- **Caveat.** In the MacDonald RCT, PROMs did not differ even though recurrence was about four times higher with Bankart alone. WOSI is therefore a weak discriminator between procedures that both work. Recurrence is the more meaningful endpoint, but it needs a larger sample.

### 4b. Recurrence (two-proportion test, two-sided α 0.05, power 80%)

| Scenario (control vs BR+R) | Rates | n per group |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated BR vs BR+R (RCT, 2 y) | 18% vs 4% | **78** |
| Isolated BR vs BR+R (RCT, ~4 y redislocation) | 22% vs 8% | **101** |
| Isolated BR vs BR+R (RCT, ~4 y overall instability) | 30% vs 10% | **62** |
| Pooled isolated BR vs a good BR+R result | 15% vs 5% | **141** |
| Pooled isolated BR (13%) vs BR+R | 12% vs 5% | **248** |
| BR+R vs LAT in off-track GBL >15% (Yang 2018 effect) | 28.6% vs 6.1% | **44** |

**Non-inferiority of BR+R vs Latarjet** (the comparison that matches the hypothesis "Latarjet is not needed"). This uses one-sided α 0.05, power 80%, and assumes equal true recurrence:

| True recurrence in both arms | NI margin | n per group |
|---|---|---|
| 6% | 10 percentage points | **70** |
| 6% | 8 percentage points | **109** |
| 8% | 10 percentage points | **92** |
| 6% | 5 percentage points | **279** |

Assumptions and recommendation:
- These are simple, unadjusted calculations.
- A retrospective cohort needs inflation for confounding adjustment (propensity score or multivariable model). Add about 10–20% for loss to follow-up.
- Use time-to-event (Kaplan-Meier/Cox) analysis, because recurrence keeps accruing after 2 years: Woodmass 2024 showed it rising from 18% to 22% with longer follow-up.
- A single-arm alternative is to benchmark the BRUMA + remplissage cohort against literature rates. For example, to show recurrence <10% when the expected rate is 5%, exact binomial with power 80%, you need roughly 150–200 shoulders. That is an estimate and should be recomputed formally.
- **Suggested primary framing:** recurrence (dislocation, or ≥2 subluxations, as in the RCT definition) stratified by GBL band (<13.5%, 13.5–20%, >20%) and track status (on, near/peripheral, off). WOSI and ER (at side and in 90° abduction) would be secondary outcomes.
