# 03 — Technique comparison literature and methodological expectations

Study being planned: retrospective comparative cohort, arthroscopic Bankart repair with knotless anchors (2014–2019, ~51 patients, 13 with remplissage) vs BRUMA (Bankart Repair Using modified Mason-Allen suture configuration, mostly all-suture anchors, 2018–2024, ~73 patients, 48 with remplissage).

Search date: 2026-10-07. Sources: PubMed (via the PubMed tool; every PMID and DOI below came back from it), plus one web search for the AJSM author instructions. Quotations are kept under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase. Anything I could not check is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

---

## 1. Summary

Three recent syntheses agree that knotless and knotted anchors give the same redislocation, revision and Rowe outcomes after arthroscopic Bankart repair. Wang 2025 pooled 3 RCTs. An OJSM 2025 meta-analysis (PMID 41140547) pooled 10 studies (n=793) and found redislocation of 9.5% knotted vs 10.6% knotless, OR 0.95. The Arthroscopy 2024 systematic review found no clear difference. The only consistent knotless advantage is a slightly shorter operating time. All-suture anchors also perform the same as conventional solid anchors for recurrence and PROMs (13 studies, n=878). They make wider bone tunnels on CT, but this has not been linked to failure. The BRUMA stitch has already been tested once: a Level 2 RCT in AJSM (Park 2018, n=86) found no difference from a vertical simple stitch in labral height, redislocation (4.9% vs 4.4%) or scores. The reviewer's starting assumption will therefore be "no difference expected", and the paper has to show it can detect one or else be framed as an equivalence or safety report. The dominant problem is confounding, not the anchor. Remplissage is in 66% of the BRUMA cohort but only 25% of the knotless cohort. Remplissage reduces recurrence substantially (pooled ORs of roughly 6–9 in favour of adding it), so an unadjusted comparison would credit BRUMA with a remplissage effect. Follow-up length also differs by era, and many Bankart failures happen after year 2. The design must therefore stratify or adjust for remplissage and glenoid track, use time-to-event analysis with a common follow-up horizon, and handle era/learning-curve effects explicitly. Indian data are thin: there are single-centre Bankart±remplissage series (Manipal, Pune, Delhi), but no published kabaddi-specific instability cohort was found.

## 3. Confounders the paper must measure and adjust for

| Risk factor | Effect size (source) | How to measure in this dataset |
|---|---|---|
| Age < 20 at surgery | Pooled recurrence 27.0% vs 13.3% (<20 vs older), about 2x; multivariable ORs 1.3–14.0 (Bulleit 2024, PMID 38852707). Age ≤20 significant at 10+ y (Do 2025, PMID 40620132) | Continuous age + binary <20 |
| Contact / collision sport | Recurrence 17% vs 8% in contact vs non-contact athletes (Saleet 2025 MA, PMID 40574344). An ISIS component (Balg 2007, PMID 17998184) | Sport type and level (competitive vs recreational); code kabaddi, rugby, wrestling and football as contact; cricket fast bowler as overhead |
| Competitive sport level | Significant in 46% of studies reporting it (Bulleit 2024) | Competitive vs recreational |
| ISIS score | Score >6 gave 70% recurrence in the original series (Balg 2007). Pooled recurrence 26.0% for ISIS ≥4 vs 6.3% below 4 (Rosenberg 2021, PMID 34535554). Predictive thresholds of 2–4 (Trasolini 2021, PMID 34591717) | Compute for all patients; use as a single composite covariate when events are few |
| Number of preoperative dislocations | Failure 24.2% after multiple events vs 13.8% after first-time dislocation (NS, Do 2025). Significant in 31% of studies (Bulleit 2024) | Count; also time from first dislocation to surgery |
| Hyperlaxity | Significant in 46% of studies (Bulleit 2024). OR 2.68 for recurrence after first-time dislocation (Olds 2015, PMID 25900943) | ER at side >85° or Gagey sign, if recorded |
| Glenoid bone loss | Multivariable ORs 0.7–35.1, 6 of 9 significant (Bulleit 2024). Failures had 24.7% vs 12.8% GBL; bone loss >13.5% worsens WOSI even without recurrence (Shaha 2015, PMID 25883168) | CT best-fit circle %; report the >13.5% threshold |
| Off-track Hill-Sachs | Multivariable OR 2.9–8.9 (Bulleit 2024). Glenoid track concept: Di Giacomo 2014 (PMID 24384275) | CT glenoid track. **Essential, because it drives the remplissage indication** |
| Remplissage | See 2c: strongly protective | Treatment covariate; stratify |
| Number of anchors | 2 anchors 25.0% vs ≥3 anchors 18.1% (P=.06) (Bulleit 2024) | Count per patient (likely differs by era) |
| Lesion type (ALPSA, bony Bankart, GLAD) | ALPSA dislocation 32.0% vs 13.3% (Arner 2022, PMID 35416079). ALPSA failures happened earlier, 1.7 vs 3.1 y (Hoyt 2025, PMID 39741482) | Arthroscopic lesion classification |
| Sex | Male OR 3.18 after first dislocation (Olds 2015); significant in 7% of post-Bankart studies (Bulleit) | Binary |
| Follow-up duration | 20% of first recurrences after arthroscopic Bankart came at ≥91 months (Zimmermann 2016, PMID 27926676). Most instability episodes came after year 2 in Kartus 2008 (PMID 18829939) | Time-to-event analysis; never compare crude proportions across unequal follow-up |

Trasolini 2021 (PMID 34591717) is the paper reviewers will use to check this: it showed that most risk-factor studies fail to control for known confounders, and it recommends reporting off-track status, GBL by advanced imaging, ISIS, sport level, anchor number and age in every study.

## 4. Example retrospective two-technique Bankart comparisons and their design choices

1. **Lee et al., Arthroscopy 2019 (PMID 30987905).** Assigned groups by the period when each anchor was in use (an era design, exactly like this study). Did an a priori power calculation on the Rowe score. Fixed the 2-year clinical endpoint and added CT arthrography at 1 year for tunnel widening. Level III.
2. **Minkus et al., J Clin Med 2024 (PMID 38592204).** Prospective consecutive new-technique cohort against a **historical control** treated for the same indication. Used co-primary endpoints (recurrence and WOSI at 24 months) and adjusted both with logistic and linear regression. With 6 events the adjusted OR CI ran from 0.89 to 115, which shows what happens to small-event multivariable models. Its message is a "tendency", not a conclusion.
3. **Siripipattanamongkol et al., APSMART 2020 (PMID 32913713).** Retrospective single-institution **matched cohort** (2009–2017) comparing a Mason-Allen construct with simple stitches. Minimum 2-year follow-up. Recorded GBL, Hill-Sachs width and glenoid track, and ran a risk-factor analysis for failure. This is the closest design template for this study.
4. **Uluyardımcı et al., Jt Dis Relat Surg 2021 (PMID 33463424).** Groups again defined by the period of anchor use. Reported redislocation and positive apprehension separately, and added Samilson-Prieto arthropathy grading.
5. **Rieussec et al., AJSM 2025 (PMID 39838910).** Not anchor-versus-anchor, but a good model of **1:1 propensity matching**, Kaplan-Meier survivorship and logistic regression in a single-centre retrospective instability cohort. Loss to follow-up was reported as 11.4%.
6. **Pandey et al., JSES 2020 (PMID 31447283).** Retrospective Bankart ± remplissage at an Indian centre. Subgroups by CT on/off-track status, minimum 2-year follow-up. Shows how to stratify by glenoid track, which this study needs.

Common features reviewers expect: Level III label, minimum 2-year follow-up for every patient, consecutive enrolment with explicit exclusions (revision surgery, GBL above a set threshold, MDI), CT-based bone measurement, recurrence split into dislocation / subluxation / apprehension / revision, and at least one validated instability PROM (WOSI or Rowe) with MCID.

## 5. How to design this study so reviewers accept it

1. **Frame the question honestly.** An RCT has already found BRUMA equal to simple stitches (Park 2019). The anchor comparison is also equivalence territory (three syntheses). So pitch the paper as "a technique package (BRUMA + all-suture anchors ± remplissage) vs knotless repair in one surgeon's practice", not as an isolated test of the stitch. State plainly that anchor type, stitch configuration and era are bundled together and cannot be separated.
2. **Deal with the remplissage imbalance first.** It is 25% in one cohort and 66% in the other. Pre-specify:
   (a) a primary analysis adjusted for remplissage and for on/off-track status;
   (b) stratified results for Bankart-only patients (~38 knotless vs ~25 BRUMA) and for Bankart + remplissage patients;
   (c) a statement of the remplissage indication rule in each era. If the rule moved from off-track only to some on-track cases, say so. Reviewers will call it confounding by indication.
3. **Use the overlap years.** Both techniques were used in 2018–2019. A sensitivity analysis limited to concurrently treated patients reduces era and learning-curve bias. Also add operation year or case sequence number as a covariate, or show recurrence by year.
4. **Equalise follow-up.**
   - Require at least 2 years for every patient. The BRUMA cases operated in late 2024 will not reach 2 years until late 2026, so check eligibility dates.
   - Make Kaplan-Meier / Cox time-to-recurrence the primary analysis.
   - Report recurrence at a fixed 2-year horizon in both groups. The knotless cohort's longer follow-up will otherwise inflate its crude rate (Zimmermann 2016; Kartus 2008).
5. **Define recurrence the way the literature does.** Report each separately and then as a composite:
   - Primary: redislocation (documented reduction or radiograph).
   - Secondary: subluxation (patient-reported), positive apprehension, revision for instability, and a composite "any instability" endpoint (as in Hoyt 2025).
   - Give the follow-up method (clinic visit vs telephone), because telephone follow-up under-captures apprehension.
6. **Respect the event count.** With ~124 patients and roughly 10% recurrence, expect about 12 events.
   - That supports only about one covariate per 10 events in conventional logistic or Cox regression.
   - Options: propensity-score IPTW or matching on age, sport, ISIS, GBL, track status and remplissage; Firth-penalised regression; or ISIS plus remplissage as the only covariates.
   - Detecting 15% vs 5% recurrence (α=.05, 80% power) needs about 140 patients per group (my calculation, normal approximation). So state that the study is underpowered for recurrence superiority. Make PROMs (WOSI/Rowe against MCID) a co-primary endpoint, or frame the recurrence result descriptively or as non-inferiority with a pre-stated margin.
7. **Report every covariate in Section 3 for both cohorts** (Table 1 with standardised mean differences), following Trasolini 2021.
8. **Measure the surgical variables directly:** number of anchors, anchor type and size, number of BRUMA vs simple stitches per repair, capsular shift, and the number of remplissage anchors (more remplissage anchors predicted ER deficit, Haikal 2025, PMID 39889946). Fewer anchors in the early knotless era would itself confound the comparison (Bulleit 2024).
9. **Imaging, if it exists:** CT arthrogram or MRI for peri-anchor cysts and tunnel widening, since all-suture anchors widen tunnels (Lee 2019; Jin 2020). Optional, but it answers the obvious reviewer question.
10. **Reporting:**
    - STROBE cohort checklist (von Elm 2007, PMID 17947786). AJSM's author instructions say a STROBE checklist must be uploaded as a supplement and a flow diagram included for cohort studies (web search of journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/AJS, 2026-10-07).
    - Requirements for OJSM, Arthroscopy, JSES and KSSTA were not checked: **UNVERIFIED**, so read each journal's author instructions before submission. OJSM is a SAGE/AOSSM sister journal and likely follows similar rules, but this is unconfirmed.
    - Flow diagram showing operated, eligible, lost to follow-up and analysed patients. Keep loss to follow-up under about 15–20% and compare responders with non-responders.
    - Ethics approval with a waiver of consent for retrospective data. Level III.

## 6. Indian / South Asian context (for the Discussion)

- **Pandey et al., JSES 2020 (Manipal; PMID 31447283):** 136 patients with GBL <25%. Off-track Bankart without remplissage redislocated more, remplissage reduced this, and it cost range of motion. This is the most relevant Indian comparator.
- **Pathak et al., Arthrosc Sports Med Rehabil 2022 (Pune; PMID 35494301):** 24 Bankart + remplissage patients with engaging HSL and GBL <20%. Mean age 30, 92% male, 4.2% failure (one positive apprehension test).
- **Sharma et al., Malays Orthop J 2025 (Delhi; PMID 40852110):** 36 manual workers with Bankart + remplissage, mean GBL 11.75%, all with engaging HSL, no dislocation at 12 months. Supports an argument that Indian surgical cohorts include labourers with overhead occupational load, not only athletes.
- **Mishra et al., Indian J Orthop 2012 (Delhi; PMID 23325970):** 50 patients, mean age 26.8, 27-month follow-up, no redislocations, 3 with laxity. An early-era Indian benchmark.
- **Sinha et al., Indian J Orthop 2021 (Delhi; PMID 34306548):** 42 non-contact athletes revised with Bankart + remplissage after a failed primary Bankart. Failure came at a mean 7.3 months after the primary surgery.
- **Nimse et al., Indian J Orthop 2023 (PMID 37766945):** review of return-to-play rehabilitation for cricket fast bowlers after Bankart repair. Useful for the overhead/throwing subgroup.
- **Samant & Wade, J Orthop 2023 (Mumbai; PMID 37928049):** 30 patients, subscapularis-augmented vs conventional Bankart for GBL <20%. Another Indian retrospective two-technique comparison.
- **Kabaddi:** PubMed has no kabaddi-specific shoulder instability cohort. The only hit was a case report of posterior sternoclavicular dislocation in a kabaddi player (Katyal 2026, PMID 42713299). This is a publishable gap: if the cohort contains kabaddi players, report them as a separate contact-sport subgroup. Any claim about kabaddi injury epidemiology from non-PubMed sources is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Two general points for the Discussion, both inferred from these series rather than from a pooled South Asian dataset: Indian series are mostly male and in their mid-20s to 30, and presentation is often delayed after multiple dislocations, which raises GBL and the share of off-track lesions.
