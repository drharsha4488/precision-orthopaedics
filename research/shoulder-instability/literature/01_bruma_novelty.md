# 01 — BRUMA (Mason-Allen Bankart) + remplissage: novelty assessment

Search date: 2026-10-07. Sources: PubMed (E-utilities via the healthcare PubMed tool), PMC full text, Consensus, web search.
Rule followed: every citation below has a PMID and/or DOI that was actually retrieved. Anything not verified is marked UNVERIFIED.

---

## 1. Summary verdict on novelty

The **technique itself is not new**. Park JY's group in Seoul (Neon Orthopaedic Clinic and Konkuk University) coined "BRUMA" in a 2019 AJSM randomised trial against simple stitches. They then published the 2026 OJSM series on small bony Bankart lesions. **That 2026 series already used remplissage in 15 of 32 patients (46.9%) and reports an exploratory with/without-remplissage subgroup.** So "BRUMA + remplissage" cannot be claimed as a first description. The authors are Korean, not from Hyderabad or India.

What **has not been published**, as far as these searches could find, is:
- a comparison of a Mason-Allen-configured Bankart repair (± remplissage) against a **knotless-anchor** Bankart repair;
- results stratified by **glenoid bone loss / glenoid track** (on-track vs off-track, subcritical bone-loss bands);
- **conversion to, or avoidance of, bone-block surgery (Latarjet)** as an endpoint;
- a cohort covering **both soft-tissue and bony Bankart lesions** with remplissage as a deliberate protocol.

The planned study is therefore novel as a **comparative, bone-loss-stratified cohort with a bone-block-avoidance endpoint**, not as a technique paper. It should cite Park 2019, Jung 2026, Siripipattanamongkol 2020 and Lai 2019 as prior art and present itself as the next step.

## 2. The BRUMA paper in detail

**Jung HS, Park HK, Oh KS, Park JY.** Clinical and Radiologic Outcomes of the Bankart Repair Using Mason-Allen (BRUMA) Technique for Small Bony Bankart Lesions: An Objective, Performance Criteria-Referenced, Single-Surgeon Case Series. *Orthop J Sports Med.* 2026 Jun 19;14(6):23259671261442219. PMID **42367312**, PMCID PMC13305377, DOI **10.1177/23259671261442219**. (Full text read via PMC.)

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Authors / institution | Center for Shoulder, Elbow and Sports Medicine, Neon Orthopaedic Clinic, Seoul, Korea (Jung, Park HK, Park JY). Konkuk University Hospital, Seoul (Oh). **Not Hyderabad / India; not the surgeon's own group.** Single senior surgeon: J.Y. Park. |
| Design | Retrospective single-arm case series, Level IV. Benchmarked against literature-derived "objective performance criteria" (OPC) from 10 published arthroscopic bony-Bankart studies. Bone-block procedures were excluded from the benchmark. |
| Period / n | Operations July 2014 – Oct 2022. 168 BRUMA cases screened, **32 included**. |
| Indication | **Only small bony Bankart lesions.** Inclusion required recurrent anterior instability, glenoid bone loss <25% on 3D-CT and an identifiable small bony fragment. Shoulders with no identifiable fragment were **excluded**, so pure soft-tissue Bankart lesions are not in this paper. Mean preoperative defect was 13.2% ± 6.0%; mean fragment size was 5.1% ± 3.6%. |
| Technique | Lateral decubitus. The capsulolabral complex is mobilised. **The fragment is neither fixed nor excised**; it is reduced indirectly by re-tensioning the labrum. Four anchors are placed from 5:30 to 2 o'clock (PressFT, CONMED; or Gryphon, DePuy Mitek). Sutures pass through the labrum only, in a modified Mason-Allen pattern: a horizontal mattress plus a simple stitch, giving a "rip-stop" effect against cut-through. These are knotted, double-strand anchors, not knotless. Reduction is judged by restored bumper height. |
| Remplissage | **Yes.** Done in **15/32 (46.9%)** whenever an off-track or engaging Hill-Sachs lesion was found at arthroscopy. |
| Rehab | Brace for about 6 weeks. Return to sport at about 6–12 months. |
| Follow-up | Mean 21.5 ± 8.5 months (range 12–50). This is short. |
| Union | **93.8% (30/32)** on 3D-CT; 95% CI 79.2–99.2%. **The ~93.8% figure is verified.** |
| Recurrence | **6.3% (2/32)**; 95% CI 0.8–20.8%. One was early motion and one was a sports reinjury; neither had revision. **The ~6.3% figure is verified.** The definition was broad: dislocation, or subluxation with a positive apprehension sign. |
| PROMs | Rowe went from 28.8 to 99.2 (Δ +70.5, above the OPC of 54.1). ASES went from 65.9 to 85.8 (Δ +19.9, **below** the OPC of 34.2). 68% of patients exceeded the ASES MCID. Glenoid defect went from 13.2% to 4.3% (Δ −8.9 points, non-inferior to the OPC). No WOSI, return-to-sport or satisfaction data. |
| OPC verdict | Rowe was superior. Defect reduction was non-inferior. **Recurrence and union did NOT meet the CI-based OPC thresholds**; union passed only on a Wilson-interval sensitivity analysis. ASES did not meet its threshold. |
| Remplissage subgroup | n=15 with remplissage vs n=17 without. Rowe gain was 75.0 vs 66.5 (p=.042). There was no difference in ASES gain or defect reduction. Recurrence rates were identical. The authors call this exploratory. |
| Authors' stated limitations | Retrospective, no control group, small n, short follow-up, and **remplissage confounds the attribution of stability to BRUMA**. |

Points relevant to the planned paper:
1. The Korean authors themselves say they need comparative studies and longer follow-up.
2. They have no knotless comparator.
3. They do not stratify by glenoid track, and they do not report Latarjet conversion.

## 4. Biomechanics: Mason-Allen vs simple/mattress

**Origin of the stitch (rotator cuff, open):**
- Gerber C et al., *J Bone Joint Surg Br* 1994;76(3):371-80, PMID **8175836** (no DOI returned). Sheep infraspinatus model. Two modified Mason-Allen stitches failed at about 359 N. Two simple stitches failed at 184 N and four at 208 N. This is the basis of the "superior grip" claim.
- Schneeberger AG, Beck M, Gerber C, *Orthopade* 1995, PMID 7617383 (German). Mattress stitch about 269 N vs Mason-Allen about 359 N, with no relevant gapping.
- Gerber C et al., *J Bone Joint Surg Am* 1999;81(9):1281-90, PMID **10505524**, DOI 10.2106/00004623-199909000-00009. In vivo in sheep. The modified Mason-Allen stitch rarely pulled through tendon, while simple stitches always did. It caused no tendon necrosis.

**Arthroscopic, anchor-based (the relevant caveat):**
- Schneeberger AG … Gerber C, *J Bone Joint Surg Am* 2002;84(12):2152-60, PMID **12473702**, DOI 10.2106/00004623-200212000-00005. Done arthroscopically with anchors, Mason-Allen was **not stronger** than a mattress stitch. On a single anchor it was weaker (168 vs 228 N), probably because the two limbs were loaded unequally. Expect reviewers to raise this.

**Bankart-specific (simple vs mattress; no human-cadaver Mason-Allen study found):**
- Kim JH et al., *BMC Musculoskelet Disord* 2023;24:765, PMID **37759194**, DOI 10.1186/s12891-023-06864-2. Meta-analysis of 6 cadaver studies (125 shoulders). Simple, horizontal mattress and double-row did not differ in load to failure, stiffness or cyclic displacement.
- Nho SJ et al., *Am J Sports Med* 2010;38(7):1405-12, PMID **20360604**, DOI 10.1177/0363546509359069. Simple, mattress and double-loaded repairs performed alike. **The knotless anchor had a lower ultimate load (about 104 N vs 184–217 N) and displaced 2 mm at a lower load.** This is the biomechanical argument against the knotless control arm.
- Lacheta L et al., *Arthroscopy* 2020;36(6):1523-32, PMID **32057982**, DOI 10.1016/j.arthro.2020.01.046. 1.8-mm all-suture anchors. Knotless and knotted were equivalent in strength, and simple and mattress were equivalent. Knotless-mattress gave the lowest capsular strain. Knotted anchors slipped more (30% vs 11%). Mattress stitches had less soft-tissue failure (36% vs 47%).
- Judson CH et al., *Am J Sports Med* 2017;45(13):3004-9, PMID **28777665**, DOI 10.1177/0363546517717671. Simple and mattress covered a similar share of the footprint (38% vs 33%). Double-row covered about 73–77%. No strength differences.
- Hagstrom LS, Marzo JM, *Arthroscopy* 2013;29(2):325-9, PMID **23290185**, DOI 10.1016/j.arthro.2012.08.025. Mattress restored labral height; simple stitches lost 1.1–2.1 mm.
- Miskovsky SN et al., *OJSM* 2020;8(8):2325967120942133, PMID **32864384**, DOI 10.1177/2325967120942133. 48 cadavers. Simple repairs failed through the labrum, mattress repairs through the capsule. Simple repairs failed more often at the inferior (6 o'clock) anchor.
- Hanna et al., *Orthopedics* 2015, PMID 25665106, DOI 10.3928/01477447-20150204-50. Simple vs vertical mattress: no difference in contact pressure, contact area or load to failure.

**Net biomechanical position:** mattress-type and Mason-Allen-type configurations give more labral height and fail in the capsule rather than the labrum. However, **no Bankart study shows higher load to failure**. The arthroscopic anchor-based Mason-Allen may even be weaker per anchor (Schneeberger 2002). A "superior soft-tissue repair" hypothesis should therefore rest on clinical endpoints (recurrence, labral height, bone-block avoidance), not on claimed strength. No human-cadaver Mason-Allen vs knotless Bankart study was found; that is a separate opportunity.

## 5. Gaps the planned study would fill

1. **First comparison of a Mason-Allen-configured Bankart repair against knotless-anchor Bankart.** All prior Mason-Allen comparisons used simple knotted stitches as the control (Park 2019, Siripipattanamongkol 2020, Lai 2019).
2. **Remplissage as a protocol arm rather than a confounder.** Jung 2026 treated remplissage as a confounder, with 15 vs 17 patients and no power. A cohort with systematic remplissage, compared against knotless ± remplissage, addresses this directly.
3. **Stratification by bone loss and glenoid track** (e.g. <13.5% / 13.5–20% / >20%; on-track vs off-track). This is not reported in any Mason-Allen paper.
4. **Bone-block avoidance endpoint**: the proportion who would have met Latarjet criteria, or who later converted. This is absent from all Mason-Allen papers.
5. **Soft-tissue and bony Bankart together.** Jung 2026 excluded pure soft-tissue lesions, while Park 2019 did not focus on bony lesions.
6. **Longer follow-up** than 21.5 months, plus WOSI and return to sport. Jung 2026 lists these as missing.
7. **First non-East-Asian (Indian) cohort.** All BRUMA/hybrid data come from Korea, Thailand and Singapore. This is a minor point but useful for an international journal.

**Prior art to acknowledge, so as not to over-claim:** Park 2019 (RCT, acronym), Jung 2026 (BRUMA + remplissage subgroup), Siripipattanamongkol 2020, Lai 2019 / Lee 2013, Housset 2021.

**Design warnings:**
- Comparing a later era against an earlier one confounds technique with learning curve, remplissage policy and anchor type. Pre-specify era adjustment or a matched/propensity analysis.
- Park's RCT found **no** clinical superiority over simple stitches. Frame the hypothesis as "BRUMA + remplissage is at least as good, with lower recurrence or bone-block need in subcritical bone loss", rather than assuming superiority.
